# Apprentice explainer: director notes

## Visual language

The whole film happens on one sheet of off-white paper (#FAF8F3) in one fixed layout, so the viewer learns the map once: the app on the left, the dashed Apprentice box in the middle, a dashed "hosted" line at x 70%, and the teacher beyond it on the right. The cast never changes shape. The request card is a white 120x80 index card with an ink outline and three squiggle lines for the question; answers are written onto it as green lines (student) or indigo lines (teacher). The student is a small green figure at a small desk with a laptop ("Qwen3 1.7B"), a stopwatch and a "thinking" toggle. The teacher is the same simple body (rounded rectangle and circle head, no faces) at twice the size, in indigo, behind a wide desk. The judge is an amber podium with a magnifying glass, a 0 to 1 dial gauge with one notch at 0.8, and a rubber stamp that prints green APPROVED or red REJECTED. Memory is an orange filing cabinet under the student; each saved card gets a row of 6 orange dots as its fingerprint, and matching means dots lining up. The cost meter is a small amber-framed taxi meter, always in the top-right corner, and it only clicks when a card reaches the teacher. Routes are thin ink lines with small dots that get re-inked green or indigo as cards travel. All strokes use one weight, all shapes are flat with no glows, gradients or particles, and on-screen text is limited to labels and numbers. B-roll sits inside a plain ink-drawn window frame on the same paper, with a slow push-in and nothing laid over the capture. Annotations go in the paper margin.

## Narration (voice af_heart)

Your app sends every question to a big A I model. Most are easy. You pay big-model prices anyway. What if a small model took the easy ones, and learned the hard ones? That's Apprentice: an open-source gateway that speaks the Open A I format. Change one base U R L, and your app never knows. First stop, the student: Qwen three, one point seven billion parameters, running locally through Ollama. Free, fast, private. Small models slip up, so a judge checks every answer: Kev, a tiny open model we fine-tuned on Colab, from under half right to over ninety-nine percent. Kev scores its confidence. Zero point eight or higher, the answer goes straight back. Lower, and it escalates to the teacher: G L M five point two, a big open-weight model. The trick: every teacher answer goes into memory. When a similar question arrives, the student gets that worked example and usually solves it alone. So the student takes more traffic over time, and the bill shrinks. We ran eighty held-out text to S Q L questions. New student: twenty-five percent. Teacher: one hundred. Apprentice, after learning: ninety-seven and a half. The student handled eighty-six percent of requests, and the bill was eighty-seven percent smaller. The catch: on question shapes it has never seen, Apprentice gets fifty-three percent, the student alone thirty-three. Memory works best on repeats. Real work repeats. A shop owner asking about orders in Hinglish. A support bot handling refunds. A coding agent making easy calls. When the student answers, your data stays local. Apprentice. Open source, open models, M I T licensed.
